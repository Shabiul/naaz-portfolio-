An "AI employee" is an AI agent given a defined job — answering enquiries, qualifying leads, booking appointments, making follow-up calls — with access to your information and the tools it needs. It is not a robot; it is software that does one role well, all day and all night.

What it can’t — and shouldn’t — do

  • Close complex negotiations. That stays human.
  • Make judgement calls with real consequences: refunds, complaints, exceptions.
  • Know things you never told it. It answers from your documents, not guesses.

A well-built AI employee knows these limits and hands the conversation to a person, with everything said so far attached.

How to hire one

  1. Pick one role with a clear, measurable job.
  2. Give it your real documents: price list, policies, FAQs.
  3. Decide when it must hand over to a person.
  4. Measure it like a new hire: reply time, leads captured, bookings made.

AI employee vs chatbot vs virtual assistant

  • Chatbot — follows a fixed menu of buttons and replies.
  • Virtual assistant — a person working remotely on your tasks.
  • AI employee — software that understands free-text and voice, answers from your documents, and acts: logs the lead, books the slot, schedules the follow-up call.

What an AI employee needs from you

  • Your price list, services, policies and FAQs, kept up to date.
  • Clear rules on what it may promise and what needs a person.
  • Access to the tools it acts in — calendar, CRM, WhatsApp number.
  • Someone who reviews its conversations in the first weeks.

Measuring an AI employee

Judge it like any new hire, on outcomes: first-reply time, share of enquiries answered without a person, leads captured, appointments booked, and customer complaints. If the numbers are good and complaints are rare, widen its job.
