Off-the-shelf or custom?

Off-the-shelf ERP and CRM products are excellent when your process looks like the industry standard. Custom management software makes sense when:

  • your process is specific and generic modules fight it;
  • per-user licence fees grow faster than the business;
  • you need it tightly connected to your website, WhatsApp and accounting software;
  • you want to own the system and the data.

Start with one module

The ERP projects that fail try to replace everything at once. Start with the module that hurts most — often orders and inventory, or leads and follow-ups — and add the next once the first is in daily use.

Signs you have outgrown spreadsheets

  • Two people keep two versions of the same sheet, and they disagree.
  • Stock counts are only right after a manual check.
  • You can’t tell what a customer ordered last time without asking someone.
  • Month-end takes days of copying between tools.
  • Nobody can see today’s numbers without asking the owner.

ERP modules, and which to build first

A full ERP covers sales orders, purchasing, inventory, production, accounts and HR. Few small businesses need all of it on day one. Pick the module where errors cost you most — usually orders and stock, or leads and follow-ups — and make that work before adding the next.

CRM, ERP or both?

A CRM looks outward, at customers and sales. An ERP looks inward, at stock, orders and money. Many growing businesses start with a CRM, then add ERP-style modules — order tracking, inventory, billing — to the same system, so customer and operations data finally live in one place.
