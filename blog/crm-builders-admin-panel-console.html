When a business says "we need a backend", it usually means one of three things — and they cost very different amounts. Getting the name right is the first step to not overpaying a CRM builder for something you didn't need, or underbuying something you did.

Admin panel, admin console, CRM — the difference

What it is forWho uses it
Admin panelEditing your website's content — pages, listings, prices, images — without a developerWhoever updates the site
Admin consoleRunning the operation behind the site — bookings, listings, enquiries, staff actions — behind a loginYour staff, daily
CRMLeads and customers over time — every enquiry, follow-up, stage in the pipeline, and who owns itSales and front desk

In practice they blur: a good admin console often includes a CRM, and a CRM usually needs an admin view. What matters is which job you are solving first.

Off-the-shelf CRM or a custom CRM builder?

Off-the-shelf CRMs are excellent when your sales process looks like everyone else's. A custom build makes sense when:

  • your process is specific — a rental fleet, a clinic's appointments, bank-auction property listings — and generic fields fight it;
  • per-user monthly fees add up faster than a one-off build would;
  • it must connect tightly to your website, WhatsApp and an AI agent;
  • you want to own the data and the system outright.

Questions we get asked

Can the CRM connect to WhatsApp?

Yes, and it should. Enquiries captured by a WhatsApp chatbot or an AI agent land in the CRM automatically.

Can we start small?

Yes. Most clients start with lead capture and follow-ups, then add pipeline stages, reporting and staff roles once the basics are in daily use.

Who owns the data?

You do. It is your system, built for your business.
