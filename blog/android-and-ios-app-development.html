An app is a big commitment: two stores, two platforms, updates forever. Done for the right reason, it is the best channel a business can own. Here is how to decide, and what Android and iOS app development involves.

Do you need an app at all?

An app earns its place when customers use it repeatedly — ordering, booking, tracking, logging in. If people visit once to find your number, a fast mobile website is better and far cheaper.

Native or cross-platform?

ApproachWhat it meansGood for
NativeSeparate apps in each platform’s own languageHeavy device features, highest performance
Cross-platformOne codebase (for example React Native or Flutter) for Android and iOSMost business apps — faster and cheaper to build and maintain

What drives the cost

  • Platforms — Android only, iOS only, or both.
  • Features — logins, payments, maps, chat, notifications, offline use.
  • The backend — the server, database and admin panel behind the app.
  • Integrations — CRM, payment gateway, WhatsApp, your existing software.

Store accounts and review

Publishing needs a Google Play developer account and an Apple Developer Program membership, each with its own fee, and both stores review apps before they go live. Apple’s review is typically stricter, so plan time for it.

Stages of mobile app development

  • Discover — who uses the app, for what, how often.
  • Design — every screen and state, agreed before code.
  • Build — the app, its backend and admin panel.
  • Test — real devices, slow networks, edge cases.
  • Launch — store listings, review, release.
  • Improve — updates driven by real usage.

Features that add cost and time

  • Real-time features — live tracking, chat.
  • Payments and wallets.
  • Offline mode and data sync.
  • Multiple user roles — customer, staff, admin.
  • Integrations with your existing software.

Mobile website, PWA or app?

A fast mobile website reaches everyone with nothing to install. A progressive web app adds app-like features from the browser. A native or cross-platform app gives the deepest device access and a place on the home screen. Start with the simplest that does the job — often a well-built website — and move up when customers ask for more.
