Every software shop now calls itself an AI company. Some build and run real AI systems; many resell someone else's chatbot with a logo on it. Neither is wrong, but you should know which one you are paying for. These are the questions we would ask if we were the client — and the ones we expect to be asked.

Ten questions to ask an AI company

  1. Can I see it working for a real client? Live systems, not a demo video. Ours are on the proof of work page.
  2. Where do the answers come from? A business AI should answer from your documents and say when it doesn't know.
  3. Which models do you use, and why? A good answer names more than one and explains the choice per task.
  4. What languages does it handle? In India, English alone rarely covers your customers.
  5. Who owns the data and the accounts? You should — your WhatsApp number, your CRM, your conversation history.
  6. Where is the data stored? Ask how they handle India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act.
  7. What happens when the AI gets it wrong? There should be a hand-off to a person, with the conversation attached.
  8. What does it cost every month, all in? Watch for usage-based API bills that appear after launch.
  9. How long until it is live? Weeks, not quarters, for a first version.
  10. What if it never goes live? Our proposals carry a written 100% refund if the system does not go live.

Red flags

  • A guaranteed result with no access to your data or process.
  • No working examples, only screenshots.
  • A contract that locks you in for a year before you have seen it work.
  • Vague answers about who can read your customers’ conversations.

Start with a pilot

The safest first project is narrow and measurable: an AI employee that answers WhatsApp enquiries, or a voice agent that follows up on leads. If it works, widen it. Our Lynq plans are built this way — monthly, no lock-in, live in seven days.

AI company, software agency or reseller?

Three kinds of business sell "AI". Resellers put their name on another company's chatbot and set it up for you — quick and cheap, but you are renting a product you can't change. Software agencies build websites and apps and add an AI feature on top. AI companies design the system around the AI: what it reads, what it is allowed to do, when it hands over, and how it is measured.

None of these is wrong. The mistake is paying AI-company prices for a reseller setup, or expecting a reseller to adapt to a process that is unusual.

What a good first engagement looks like

  • A written scope — what the AI will and won't do, in plain language.
  • Your documents first — price lists, policies and FAQs gathered before anything is built.
  • A hand-off rule — exactly when a person takes over, and how they are told.
  • A success measure agreed up front: reply time, leads captured, bookings made.
  • A review after two weeks of real conversations, with fixes.

Data and privacy

An AI system reads your customers’ messages, so treat the choice of AI company like choosing who holds your customer list. Ask where data is stored, who at the company can read conversations, how long it is kept, and how you get it back if you leave. Questions we get asked

Is an AI company different from an AI agency?

The words overlap. What matters is whether they build and run systems against your business, as covered in what an AI agency actually builds.

Do I need my own AI model?

Almost never. Well-chosen existing models, grounded in your documents, cover nearly every business use.

How much does an AI company charge in India?

It ranges from a monthly subscription to a one-off build. Our Lynq plans start at ₹11,999/month; custom multi-agent systems are priced on complexity.

Can I switch AI companies later?

You should be able to — which is why owning your number, your data and your CRM matters from day one.
