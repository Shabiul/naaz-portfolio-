"Website builders" covers two different things: drag-and-drop platforms you run yourself, and the teams who build websites for you. We are the second kind, so take this with that in mind — but we will tell you plainly when a DIY builder is the right call, because sometimes it is.

Where DIY website builders win

  • Speed to a first version. A template and an afternoon can get a simple brochure site online.
  • Low starting cost. You pay a monthly plan instead of a project fee.
  • You edit everything yourself, with no one to wait on.

If you need five pages, a contact form and nothing else for the next year, a builder is a perfectly sensible choice.

Where they stop

  • Business logic. Fare calculators, room availability, multi-step booking, listings with filters — the things that make a site actually sell — quickly outgrow templates.
  • Connections. Sending enquiries to WhatsApp and into a CRM, or putting an AI agent behind the chat, usually needs custom work anyway.
  • Speed and search. Heavier template sites can be slower, and you control less of the code that search engines read.
  • Ownership. You rent the platform. Leave it, and the site does not come with you.

A quick way to decide

Choose a DIY builder if all of these are true:

  • the site is mainly information and a contact form;
  • you are happy to maintain it yourself;
  • search ranking and speed are not your main source of customers.

Choose a custom website if customers book, buy, search listings or enquire through it, if it has to talk to WhatsApp or a CRM, or if you want to own it.
