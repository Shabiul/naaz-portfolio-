What to ask any web developer

  • Show me three live sites you built, and let me speak to one client.
  • What will it be built with, and can another developer take it over?
  • Who owns the code, the domain and the hosting account?
  • How will it load on a cheap phone on a slow connection?
  • What does support cost after launch?

What actually drives web development cost

  • Number of unique page designs, not number of pages — ten pages on one template cost less than four bespoke ones.
  • Business logic — booking flows, fare calculators, search filters, logins.
  • Content — who writes the copy and supplies photos.
  • Integrations — WhatsApp, CRM, payment gateway, Google Business Profile.
  • An admin panel — so you can edit content yourself.

A web development brief that gets you accurate quotes

Before you ask any web developer for a price, write one page that answers: what the site must make customers do (call, book, buy, enquire), which pages you need, who your customers are, three sites you like and why, and what the site must connect to. A clear brief turns vague estimates into comparable quotes.

Signs of good web developers

  • They ask about your business before they talk about technology.
  • They show live sites, not just screenshots — and those sites load fast on a phone.
  • They explain what happens after launch: hosting, backups, updates, support.
  • They put the scope, timeline and payment milestones in writing.
